Change settings

What changing something does

A settings change makes a new version, stamped with the moment it takes effect. Every assessment records which version it ran under. Assessments made before your change keep running on the version that was in force then — what the app told you in the past stays true to what it knew at the time.

Editing your target range reclassifies where you sit relative to it, and nothing else. Your trend, what your tank consumes and your solution’s strength are properties of the tank, not of your preferences, and they do not move.

The one thing this cannot do: replay an assessment from before your very first settings version. There is nothing to replay it against, so the app refuses rather than assuming today’s settings applied back then. CONFIG_HISTORICAL_UNAVAILABLE

Learning is switched off in this build

The app could, in principle, watch what actually happens when you change the dose and work out how strong your solution really is. That is deliberately disabled here, and it ships disabled — the recipe figure above is used for every calculation.

Why: learning needs three facts recorded reliably before it can be trusted, and the app is not yet capturing them.

M-2 · which batch of solution was in useNot captured
M-3 · which pump setup was in useNot captured
M-9 · the confirmed rate before and after each changeNot captured

What this costs you: nothing about the core advice. Position, trend, consumption, dose recommendations and safety returns all run normally on the recipe figure. What you lose is the app noticing that your actual solution is, say, 15% weaker than the recipe says.

Tools

Coming later

Calculators — work out a dose by hand, convert units, plan a water change — will live in their own tab. They are not here yet, and the tab is deliberately in place and disabled rather than absent.

The reason is that a calculator answering a question you asked and a recommendation the engine decided to make are different kinds of statement, and they must never be mistaken for one another. Deciding that now means it does not have to be untangled later.

Open question for the owner. There is a reason code for the target range changing and one for the volume changing, but none for the outer bounds changing — even though editing them moves the safety destinations and can turn a breach into a non-breach. The change history row above is honest, but the assessment that follows it has no code to explain itself with. Recorded as gap 11.
