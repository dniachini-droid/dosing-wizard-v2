Three tiers, and what each one is worth

Snapshots On

The last 12 states of the app, kept automatically.

This is an undo history, not a backup. It lives in the same browser storage as everything else. Clearing site data, a browser eviction, or a lost phone takes the snapshots with the data. It will refuse to write an empty snapshot over a full one, so a wipe cannot quietly overwrite the last good copy — but that is the only thing it protects you from.

Write to a folder you choose Not available here

A real file on your own disk, rewritten automatically.

Chromium browsers only, which means not iOS. On an iPhone or iPad no browser can do this, whatever it is called. Said plainly rather than offered and then failing.

Send it somewhere Available

The system share sheet — email it, put it in a cloud folder, message it to yourself.

Not automatic, and the app cannot tell whether it worked. The share sheet does not report back, so a dismissal and a successful save look identical from here. The app will not claim you have a backup on the strength of it.

If your data disappears

Two checks run independently, because either one alone can be fooled.

A marker left behindPresentwritten on first run; missing means either a wipe or a genuinely new install
The shape of the storageConsistentorphaned photos with no records pointing at them is the signature of a partial wipe
The honest limit. On a full clear, both of these go too. The app then cannot tell a wiped install from a new one, and it will say so rather than guess. It will not tell you your data was lost, and it will not tell you everything is fine.
