Setting up

Four things, then you’re done

Every one of these is required. Skipping any of them means the app has to refuse part of its answer rather than guess.

Step 2 of 4

Where do you want alkalinity to sit?

Two pairs of numbers. The target range is where you want to be. The outer bounds are where the app stops offering and starts recommending an urgent return.

Target range
to dKH

Suggested 8.2 – 8.8 dKH. Edited here to 8.6 – 9.2. The suggestion is a starting point, not a rule.

Outer bounds
to dKH

Defaults 7.0 – 11.0 dKH. Outside these, the app switches to a different register entirely and recommends a safety return.

Worked out by the engine from the above, not stored: the aim point a return plan heads for is the middle of your target range — 8.90 dKH. The safety destination is the outer bound plus a fixed 0.20 dKH buffer — 7.20 low, 10.80 high. You never set these directly, and they move when you edit the ranges.
Rules the app will not bend
Minimum must be below maximumWill not save
Target range must sit inside the outer boundsWill not save
Outer minimum must be below outer maximumWill not save

Without a target range there is no position at all — not “in range”, not “out of range”, nothing. Readings are still stored.

What are you dosing?

This is what turns a dKH figure into millilitres. Without it the app can still tell you how much alkalinity you need — it just cannot tell you how much liquid that is.

Grams of dry powder per litre of finished solution. For a commercial product, enter the manufacturer’s stated strength instead.

That gives 0.0686 dKH per mL in a 77-litre system. Calculated from your recipe, not measured. The app will use this figure for every dose until you give it a reason to change.

Step 4 of 4

What can your pump actually do?

Two things: the smallest change you can set it to, and how it spreads the daily dose out.

mL/day

If your pump moves in whole millilitres, put 1. If it does tenths, put 0.1. Every recommended dose gets rounded to a multiple of this, and then re-checked against the safety limits.

How the daily dose is delivered
Hourly24 × per day Several times a day Once a day Not sure yet

Used to work out how much of a dose change had actually landed by the time you next tested. “Not sure yet” is a real answer and is recorded as unknown — it is not treated as once a day.

If this is left empty

Nothing is held back. This app never drives your pump — it tells you a number and you set it — so a rounding step is about legibility and nothing else. Leave it blank and you get the figure at full precision.

What this app does, and does not do, yet

Alkalinity is assessed end to end. Position against your target range, trend, how much your tank uses, a maintenance dose recommendation, safety returns when you go outside the outer bounds, and when to test next.

Everything else is logged and charted, and nothing more. Calcium, magnesium, nitrate, phosphate and salinity are recorded as facts. They get a value, a date and a chart. They get no status, no range position, no trend, no advice and no reminders — and they cannot affect the alkalinity advice.

The app recommends. You implement. It cannot see your tank or your pump. When it recommends a dose it will ask you afterwards whether you made the change, and “I’m not sure” is a real answer that it stores as unknown rather than converting into a yes or a no.

It will refuse rather than guess. Where a rule it needs does not exist, or a fact you have not given it is load-bearing, it says so, names what is missing, and gives you everything it can still stand behind. Refusals are normal here. A blank would be a bug.

Calculators — the sort where you type in numbers and get an answer — will live in their own Tools tab when they arrive, deliberately away from advice. A calculator answering a question you asked and a recommendation the engine chose to make are different things, and they should never look alike.
