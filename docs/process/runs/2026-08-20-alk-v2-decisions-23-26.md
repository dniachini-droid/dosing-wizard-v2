## What actually changed, in plain language

**The application does not control anything.** It never had a connection to a dosing pump
and never will. It reads what the keeper measured, works out what the tank needs, and tells
a person a number. The person decides whether to change their own equipment.

The package had been written as though that were not true. A whole layer of it existed to
manage a connection that does not exist: an "exact advisory rate" separated from an
"executable pump command"; refusals that withheld a dose because the app did not know the
pump's step size; a status meaning "I cannot command this hardware"; and — most
consequentially — reasoning that treated *withholding a recommendation* as though it caused
delivery to keep running. Owner decision 23 removes all of it.

The other three decisions are smaller and follow from the same clarity.

**Decision 24** — the advisory ceiling and floor stop being a refusal. Beyond them the
engine keeps advising by the ordinary rules and simply attaches a warning: this reading is
beyond the range I am confident in, confirm it with a second test, check the dosing setup,
and correction at this level wants experienced judgement. The number does not change at the
boundary. One increment below it and one increment above it, the engine says the same thing
with a warning added.

**Decision 25** — the refusal for an unknown delivered rate is checked **before** the engine
picks which high-breach branch to use, so it applies to all of them. Previously it was
attached to a formula, and one of the three branches does not use that quantity, so the same
tank state had two possible answers depending on how you read the rule.

**Decision 26** — the warning and the retest scheduler now say the same thing, because the
warning just prints whatever the scheduler decided.

## Decision 23 — the sweep, in full

### Concepts removed

| Concept | What it was |
|---|---|
| the actuator | there is no execution path from the engine to the tank |
| the advisory-versus-executable split | one recommended rate, not a pair |
| actuator capability | nothing is unknown about a device the app does not talk to |
| the actuator increment | replaced by a **display precision**: a rounding convention for a human reader |
| withholding as a physical event | withholding means "the engine cannot advise", not "the pump keeps running" |

### Rules retired

Both are marked `RETIRED by owner decision 23` in place, with their previous wording
preserved as a quoted block, and both are removed from the coverage manifest.

| Rule | Why | What survives, and where |
|---|---|---|
| `ALK-SAFETY-TEMP-RATE-RESOLUTION-001` | its whole premise was emitting an exact rate while the executable command was `NOT_RUN` for want of an increment | the potency requirement and the rails, restated in the rules that own them |
| `ALK-SAFETY-CORRECTION-RESOLUTION-001` | an `M-1` exemption letting a one-off correction volume survive a missing increment; there is no increment | the correction volume itself, which `ALK-OUTER-BOUND-ACTION-001` always calculated |

`M-1`'s actuator limb is retired; the rest of `ALK-CAPABILITY-CONTRACT-001` — missing
potency, missing solution or delivery context, imprecise time, unavailable historical
configuration — is untouched, because none of it is about a device.

### Reason codes retired

| Code | Replaced by |
|---|---|
| `CAPABILITY_ACTUATOR_INCREMENT_REQUIRED` | **nothing.** The state cannot arise |
| `SAFETY_TEMP_RATE_ADVISORY_EMITTED` | **nothing.** It announced one half of a pair that no longer exists |
| `SAFETY_CORRECTION_ACTIONABLE_WITHOUT_INCREMENT` | **nothing.** It announced surviving a refusal that no longer exists |
| `SAFETY_ADVISORY_RANGE_EXCEEDED` | `SAFETY_ADVISORY_CONFIDENCE_WARNING` (decision 24 — it now warns instead of withholding) |

### Reason codes renamed — substance survives, the name was wrong

| Old | New | Why the substance is independent of any device |
|---|---|---|
| `VALIDATION_ACTUATOR_INCREMENT_INVALID` | `VALIDATION_RECOMMENDATION_PRECISION_INVALID` | a configured precision ≤ 0 is still invalid |
| `MAINTENANCE_ACTUATOR_RESOLUTION` | `MAINTENANCE_ROUNDS_TO_CURRENT_DOSE` | the rounded number equalling the current dose is a fact about the number |

### Fields and symbols renamed

| Old | New |
|---|---|
| `actuatorIncrementMlPerDay` | `recommendationPrecisionMlPerDay` |
| `temporarySafetyRateAdvisoryMlPerDay` | `temporarySafetyRateContinuousMlPerDay` — an **auditable intermediate**, no longer an output |
| `temporarySafetyPumpCommandMlPerDay` | `temporarySafetyRateRecommendationMlPerDay` — **the** single output |

This mirrors the shape ordinary maintenance already had — `continuousActionCandidateMlPerDay`
beside `recommendedDoseMlPerDay` — so the safety path now looks like the maintenance path
instead of like a device driver.

### Prose swept

"actuator command", "executable command", "pump command", "actuator rounding", "actuator
resolution", "actuator-meaningful", "the maintenance actuator", "actuator/pour resolution"
and their variants are gone from the canon and the package as live vocabulary — 200-odd
occurrences across 15 files. The words survive only where a rule is **retiring** them, or
inside a preserved-history blockquote. The run logs and the never-edited implementation
handoff are deliberately not swept: they are the record of what was true when they were
written.

### Open findings that became inapplicable

**`OI-SAFETYRATE-001` — reclassified INAPPLICABLE.** It asked how a temporary safety rate
should behave when the actuator increment was unknown. Freeze 5 answered it as F5-11 by
writing `ALK-SAFETY-TEMP-RATE-RESOLUTION-001`. Both the question and the answer are gone.
This is recorded as *inapplicable*, not *resolved* — nothing decided it; it stopped existing.

**No other open finding depends on an actuator existing.** Each of the eleven remaining was
checked against the test the owner set — does its substance require an actuator, or does it

`OI-DETERMINISM-001` and `OI-BOUNDARIES-001` mention rounding or increments; all three are
about ordering, representation and comparison, none about a device.

## Decision 24 — the boundary warns

`ALK-ADVISORY-RANGE-BOUNDARY-001` is rewritten. The boundary is unchanged
(`OuterMax + 1.0 dKH`, `OuterMin − 1.0 dKH`, an offset from the configured bounds, inclusive,
exact-decimal, no epsilon). What happens at it is the opposite of before:

```text
was (decision 21):  recommendedDoseMlPerDay = WITHHELD, both rate fields NOT_RUN, escalate
now (decision 24):  the ordinary recommendation, unchanged, + advisoryConfidenceWarning
```

Decision 21's wording is preserved as a quoted block inside the rule.

The warning states five things, none optional, and **may not alter** the recommended rate,
the trajectory, the consumption estimate or the retest schedule. `AD-ESC-001` demonstrates
the point directly: at 11.9, 12.0 and 12.1 dKH the recommendation is **1.8 mL/day in all
three**, from the same rules, differing only by the warning.

**Closed by this decision:** `OI-ADVISORYEXCEPTION-001` (there is no exception list, because
there is no withholding to except anything from), `OI-ADVISORYMEMBERS-001` (the member-wise
predicate over contested-episode members is retired with the withholding it served, so the
`SUSPECT`-member question cannot arise), `OI-ADVISORYRETURN-001` (a terminated return plan
is replaced by the ordinary recommendation, as it is anywhere else).

### `OI-SIZINGFLAT-001` — confirmed affected, and NOT closed

The owner asked explicitly. **It is affected, in the unhelpful direction.**

Decision 21 had bounded the flat region by refusing to advise above `AdvisoryCeiling`.
Decision 24 removes that refusal. The ordinary rules now run at every level, so `R_down`
saturates at the 0.50 dKH/day rail and the sized rate stops responding to the measured value
from `A_safe,high + 0.50 dKH` **upward without limit** — exactly as it did before decision
21 existed. `AD-ESC-001`'s six cases all produce 1.8 mL/day, which is decision 24's property
*and* is this exposure.

The item is therefore **open and no longer narrowed**, and it is wider now than it was one
decision ago. It is **not** closed. Nothing compensates for it and no branch boundary was
moved. This is stated in the canon rule, the register entry, the algorithm contract, the
test matrix and the fixture's own `openIssues`.

## Decision 25 — one answer per state

The unknown-`D_current` refusal moves out of `ALK-HIGH-BREACH-SAFETY-SIZING-001`'s formula
clause and becomes a **precondition in `ALK-DELIVERY-RATE-BASIS-001`, evaluated before
branch selection**, applying identically to A, B and B′.

The plain problem it fixes: branch A's formula is
`max(0, (C_estimate + S_safety) / P_selected)`. It does not mention `D_current`. So a rule
saying "if `D_current` is unknown, no safety rate is emitted" had nothing to attach to on
branch A, and the same tank state — 11.5 dKH, `C_estimate` +0.60, `P` 0.0693 — gave
**1.4 mL/day** under one reading and a refusal under the other. The precondition is about
the *state*, not about which symbols a formula happens to use.

`INV-G15` asserts that no high-breach state produces both a numeric recommendation and a
refusal, and that the same state cannot give different outputs under different readings.
**Closes `OI-BRANCHAREFUSAL-001`.**

## Decision 26 — one retest answer

`ALK-RETEST-SCHEDULER-001` owns retest timing and always did. The warning now **renders
whatever the scheduler produced** and states no interval of its own; "confirm with a second
test" is a statement about confidence, not a schedule. The warning submits no candidate and
computes no next-test time, so the card and the scheduler cannot disagree.
**Closes `OI-ADVISORYRETEST-001`.**
